        Rules this tool follows

        How we choose between courses that teach similar things

        • How your six electives are chosen. Every plan has three kinds of slot, filled in this order:
          1. 3 Foundation courses: what your role is built on, decided by course content alone (not job postings). Each role has three defining skills, for example language & text, LLMs and applied NLP for an NLP Engineer, and each slot goes to the course that teaches its skill in most depth. If you aim for a specialisation, its required courses fill the foundation first and the rest come from its category, closest to your role.
          2. 1 Interest course: a starter in your one minor interest, the best-matching course on its list that fits. If a better one clashes with one of your foundation courses, and another course teaches that foundation course's skill closely enough (rated 2 or more out of 3; 2.5 or more for your first foundation course, the one the role is built on) and shares real content with it (its handout overlaps by 0.5 or more on our overlap scale), that course takes its foundation place and you get the better interest course. A course your specialisation requires is never replaced. Otherwise the foundation stays and you get the next course on the interest's list.
          3. 2 Job-market courses (3 if you pick no interest): the courses that add most of what your role's job postings ask for that the plan doesn't already cover, counting skills asked for in at least a quarter of postings. Courses on your role's list, or clearly relevant to its postings, come first. Hiring demand counts lightly. Only when the bucket rules leave no relevant course does the plan use a bucket filler.
          When slots compete for a bucket, foundation wins, then your interest, then the job market.
        • Modern content (1–5) comes from what each handout actually teaches, not when it was written. For example, LLMs for GenAI (mixture-of-experts, RLHF, agents) rates 5; Probabilistic Graphical Models (classical Bayesian networks) rates 1.
        • Job demand (1–5) is our reading of 2025–26 hiring reports (sources below). It counts only in the job-market slots, and only lightly: relevance to your role's postings comes first. It favours GenAI, agentic AI, MLOps, data and security skills, which grew fastest; robotics and AI-ethics skills are a small share of postings today.
        • Topic overlap. We compared every pair of handouts. Plans that repeat content (for example Data Mgmt for ML and MLOps both teach pipelines, MLflow and drift) are penalised, so slots go to new material and to your interests.
        • Job postings decide only the job-market slots. Each role's posting profile comes from real job postings (see the skill map). "Learning theory" counts half outside research roles, because in postings it mostly means a PhD or publications. Your foundation never depends on postings.
        • Sources for job demand: . Demand changes quickly, so these ratings are a snapshot as of October 2026.

        From the institute's documents

        • Semester 2: two compulsory courses (Artificial & Computational Intelligence, 5 units; Deep Reinforcement Learning, 4 units) plus 2 electives.
        • Semester 2 electives come in 4 buckets, and you can take only one course from a bucket.
        • Semester 3: 4 electives from 6 buckets. This list is marked tentative and will be confirmed at the Semester 3 orientation.
        • Semester 4: dissertation, 16 units. You need at least 49 units of coursework to graduate.
        • Specialisation: 3 courses from the category, including the mandatory ones. Mandatory courses are NLP (NLP); Unsupervised Deep Learning# (Deep Learning); Computer Vision and Audio Analysis# (Audio & Vision).
        • Category courses. NLP: NLP, Speech Processing, NLP Applications, Conversational AI, Social Media Analytics, LLMs for GenAI. Deep Learning: Unsupervised DL, ML System Optimization, Learning Theory, ML on the Edge, Graph NNs, Fair & Trustworthy ML. Audio & Vision: Computer Vision, Audio Analysis, Computational Photography, 3D CV, Multimodal IR. General (counts toward none): Cloud Native, Quantum ML, Robotics, MLOps.

        Assumptions and open questions

        • Semester 3 is treated as one course per bucket, like Semester 2. The documents state this rule only for Semester 2.
        • For your batch, ML System Optimization and Multimodal IR are taken in Semester 2 only, so they don't appear in Semester 3.
        • When you aim for a specialisation, only plans that qualify are recommended. ML System Optimization and Multimodal IR are counted toward Deep Learning and Audio & Vision, because the Semester 3 table lists them there, even though your batch takes them in Semester 2. Video Analysis is not listed in any category, so it isn't counted.
        • For Audio & Vision, both Computer Vision (marked mandatory on the Semester 2 slide) and Audio Analysis (#) are treated as required.
        • Architecting AI Systems has no handout. Its description comes from its title only.
        • The Video Analysis handout is an older "Video Analytics" version with a different course code (ZG531 vs ZG540).
        • Each course's topic profile was cross-checked against a keyword count of its full handout text (modules and session plans).
        • Each role's three defining skills, and so its foundation courses, are our reading of what the role does, checked against the course handouts. They are not an institute rule. Multimodal IR is named directly as a Multimodal AI Engineer's foundation, because no single skill area captures multimodal retrieval.
        • The "builds on" links come from overlapping handout topics. They are not official prerequisites; none of the electives lists another elective as a prerequisite.
        • AI/ML for Cyber Security and Cloud Native Solutions are worth 5 units instead of 4.
